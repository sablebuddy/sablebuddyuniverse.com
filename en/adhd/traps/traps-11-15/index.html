🪤 Cognitive traps 11–15

A breakdown of five cognitive traps: how they work, how to spot them, what to do — and why the brain is wired this way at all.

Fortune telling, minimization, tunnel vision, magical thinking, just world fallacy.

Traps on this page:
Fortune TellingMinimizationTunnel VisionMagical ThinkingJust World Fallacy

11. Fortune Telling

What it is

You 'know' the future (it's bad)

Example

No point trying - it won't work anyway

What to do

You're not a prophet. What do facts say? Has it worked before?

Why your brain does this

Your brain makes predictions based on past experience, but the database is skewed. Failures are stored in high-def. Successes are filed away in some drawer you never open.

12. Minimization

What it is

Shrink your achievements, magnify others'

Example

She has real talent, I just got lucky

What to do

Imagine a friend said about herself what you say about you. Would you agree?

Why your brain does this

You hold yourself to a different standard than everyone else. You're harsh with yourself because you feel responsible for your own actions. You're generous with others because you don't see their struggle. Your brain doesn't notice the double standard.

13. Tunnel Vision

What it is

Focus on one aspect, everything else vanishes

Example

One mistake in a project = entire project is a failure

What to do

Step back. Look at the whole picture. What else is there besides the mistake?

Why your brain does this

Your attention goes where anxiety is. One error lights up the alarm, and the brain throws all its resources at that spot. Everything else fades out - not because it's gone, but because your brain stopped looking.

14. Magical Thinking

What it is

'If I worry enough, it will prevent bad things'

Example

Can't stop worrying - what if something bad happens if I relax?

What to do

Worry doesn't protect. It drains energy. What can you actually do?

Why your brain does this

Your brain can't handle feeling powerless. Doing nothing feels unbearable, so it does the next best thing: it worries. Worrying feels like action. It's not - but it scratches that itch.

15. Just World Fallacy

What it is

'If I'm good enough, I'll be treated well'

Example

I tried so hard and they didn't appreciate me - it's unfair!

What to do

The world isn't fair. But you can choose who you surround yourself with.

Why your brain does this

Your brain craves predictability. It looks for patterns, for cause and effect, even when there are none. 'If I'm good, good things will happen' is your brain trying to make chaos feel manageable.
