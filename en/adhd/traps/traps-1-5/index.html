🪤 Cognitive traps 1-5

A breakdown of five cognitive traps: how they work, how to spot them, what to do - and why the brain is wired this way at all.

Catastrophizing, mind reading, black-and-white thinking, discounting the positive, emotional reasoning.

Context: Your brain is working to spec - where cognitive traps come from and why they are settings, not a malfunction.

Deeper: Your brain can't stand a blank space - catastrophizing and mind reading broken down step by step, with examples.

Deeper: Your brain cuts the world in half - black-and-white thinking broken down step by step, with the lines experiment.

1. Catastrophizing

What it is

Worst-case scenario feels like the only possible outcome

Example

She didn't reply > she hates me > I'll get fired > I'll end up homeless

What to do

Ask: what's the realistic probability? What's most likely to happen?

Why your brain does this

Your brain is wired to spot danger. Missing a real threat is more costly than a false alarm, so it defaults to worst-case. Great instinct for dodging predators. Terrible instinct for reading texts.

2. Mind Reading

What it is

You 'know' what others think (usually something bad)

Example

He looked at me that way - he definitely thinks I'm stupid

What to do

You're not a telepath. Write down: what did he DO? What did you ASSUME?

Why your brain does this

Your brain can't stand not knowing. When someone's thoughts are unclear, it fills the gap - and because the threat system runs the show, it fills it with the worst option. Just in case.

3. Black-and-White Thinking

What it is

Only two options: perfect or terrible. No middle ground

Example

If I don't do this 100%, I'm a total failure

What to do

Find a third option. 'Done at 70% - is that good enough?'

Why your brain does this

Sorting things into shades takes effort. Splitting them into good/bad is fast and cheap. Your brain takes the shortcut. Handy for survival, less handy for real life.

4. Discounting the Positive

What it is

Successes don't count, but failures are real

Example

Promotion? Just luck. But that task I failed - that's the real me

What to do

Win file. Write down every success. Reread it.

Why your brain does this

Bad memories stick harder than good ones. It's called negativity bias - your brain files threats in bold and highlights, while good stuff gets buried in the footnotes.

5. Emotional Reasoning

What it is

I feel it = it must be true

Example

I feel worthless - therefore I am worthless

What to do

Emotion is not evidence. 'I FEEL worthless' != 'I AM worthless'.

Why your brain does this

Your brain treats emotions as data - just like what you see or hear. It doesn't stop to check whether the feeling matches reality. It just reacts.
