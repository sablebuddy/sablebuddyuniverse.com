🪤 Cognitive traps 6–10

A breakdown of five cognitive traps: how they work, how to spot them, what to do — and why the brain is wired this way at all.

Should statements, labeling, personalization, overgeneralization, mental filter.

Traps on this page:
Should StatementsLabelingPersonalizationOvergeneralizationMental Filter

6. Should Statements

What it is

'Should', 'must', 'have to' - applied to yourself and others

Example

I should be organized. He should have understood

What to do

Replace 'should' with 'want to' or 'it would be nice'. See Demand Avoidance section.

Why your brain does this

These are rules you absorbed growing up - from family, school, the world around you. Your brain stored them as absolute laws because breaking them used to have consequences.

7. Labeling

What it is

Turning a behavior into a personality diagnosis

Example

Forgot a meeting = 'I'm irresponsible'. Not 'I forgot' but 'I AM like that'

What to do

Separate fact from verdict. 'I forgot' = behavior. 'Irresponsible' = label.

Why your brain does this

Your brain loves shortcuts. 'I'm forgetful' is faster to process than 'I forgot one specific thing in one specific situation.' Labels are efficient. They're also wrong.

8. Personalization

What it is

Everything that happens is somehow about you

Example

Colleague is frowning = I must have done something wrong

What to do

Not everything is about you. People have their own lives. Ask if unsure.

Why your brain does this

Your brain is constantly scanning: am I safe in this group? Anything that could mean you're losing standing gets flagged as urgent. Old wiring - back when getting kicked out of the tribe meant you were dead.

9. Overgeneralization

What it is

One incident becomes a rule

Example

One failed presentation = 'I always fail'

What to do

'Always? Really? Name three times it went OK.'

Why your brain does this

One vivid moment sticks harder than ten forgettable ones. Your brain writes rules based on what it remembers most, and what it remembers most is whatever hit hardest emotionally.

10. Mental Filter

What it is

Out of 100 events, you see only the one negative

Example

99 kind comments and 1 criticism. You remember only the criticism

What to do

Read all 100. Literally. Count the ratio.

Why your brain does this

Your brain filters by threat level. Negative stuff passes through first. It's not a choice you're making - it's how the filter is built.
